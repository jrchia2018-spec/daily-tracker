# Section 4 — Dominant story arc: IRAN WAR (mutable; state as of 24 July 2026)

- **Origin:** 28 Feb 2026 — US/Israel large-scale strikes on Iran; Supreme Leader Ali Khamenei assassinated. Iran closed Hormuz; missile/drone campaign across region.
- **MOU:** signed electronically June 17 (Trump at Versailles, Pezeshkian in Tehran). 14-point framework: 60-day ceasefire extension; Hormuz toll-free reopening; US naval blockade lifted; nuclear file deferred to Phase 2. Four working groups in Switzerland. **9 Jul: Trump declared the ceasefire "over"** after the 7 Jul Hormuz tanker attacks; the MOU's practical status has now deteriorated further with two consecutive nights of large-scale US strikes (8-9 Jul) and Iranian retaliation against four additional states (Kuwait, Qatar, Bahrain, Jordan) on 9 Jul. No formal termination notice has been filed by either side.
- **8 Jul strikes:** CENTCOM's opening wave hit ~80 military targets including 60+ IRGC fast-attack craft; Kharg Island, Bandar Abbas, Sirik and Qeshm hit. Oil: Brent +5.2% to $78.02, WTI +4.4% to $73.52.
- **9 Jul escalation — second wave and multi-country retaliation:** CENTCOM struck a further ~90 targets along Iran's coast (air defence, coastal surveillance, missile/drone storage, naval assets, logistics). Iran's Bushehr deputy governor said a US projectile hit the Bushehr nuclear plant's perimeter; the Aq Taqeh Khan railway bridge in Golestan (a China/Russia rail trade link) was struck and Tehran-Mashhad rail halted. Iran's Health Ministry: 14 killed, 78 injured (47 hospitalised) across five provinces. Iran retaliated in two phases: drone/missile attacks on a Patriot battery in Kuwait, a satellite/early-warning site in Qatar, and fuel-storage sites in Bahrain used by US forces; then 10 ballistic missiles at Jordan's Al Azraq air base (Jordan intercepted 8, no casualties). Kuwait shot down 3 ballistic missiles, a cruise missile and 10 drones (1 wounded by debris). Oil pulled back from the 8 Jul surge: Brent -$1.03 to $76.99, WTI -$0.88 to $72.64 (07:49 GMT 9 Jul) — markets still reading containment over collapse, for now. Trump says US would "probably" strike again; still floating a Kharg Island takeover / full blockade.
- **Diplomacy:** Doha indirect US-Iran technical talks July 1 — "positive progress"; talks paused for Khamenei funeral, agreed to resume "as soon as possible." Pakistan, Qatar, Egypt, Turkey, Saudi mediators continued working around the clock through 9 Jul to prevent full MOU collapse despite the wider strikes.
- **Hormuz:** traffic remains far below pre-war levels (120-140/day pre-war baseline); vessel-tracking 9 Jul showed fewer transits, activity persisting mainly on Iran-approved routes rather than the US-backed Omani corridor. ~6,000 seafarers stranded per IMO (as of 9 Jul); broader evacuation plan still paused. Iran has mined large stretches of the strait. Maersk, MSC, CMA CGM, Hapag-Lloyd still on Cape routing. ADNOC: full flows not before 2027.
- **Lebanon:** MOU calls for end of hostilities on all fronts incl. Lebanon. Israel not party; Netanyahu says forces stay "indefinitely." Israeli drone strike killed 2 in Nabatieh 8 Jul — Lebanese officials call it a fresh ceasefire violation; no sign of any enforcement mechanism changing the pattern of recurring strikes.
- **Gaza (adjacent, not MOU-governed):** ceasefire remains in place nominally but violated repeatedly; 9 Jul: at least 9 Palestinians killed in 24h incl. a World Central Kitchen aid driver (Karem Abu Salem crossing) and 4 (incl. a child) in a Khan Younis tent strike. Governance handoff (Emergency Committee dissolved 6 Jul, NCAG still outside Gaza) remains stalled independent of the violence.
- **Nuclear:** Iran holds 440.9kg HEU at 60%. IAEA access dispute unresolved — Iran's deputy FM (7 Jul) said access will be "reviewed and decided only within the framework of a final agreement" tied to full sanctions relief. No further movement reported; Phase 2 talks remain paused and now further complicated by the 8-9 Jul escalation.
- **Khamenei funeral and succession:** Najaf procession 8 Jul (~2.3M mourners per Iraq PMF), continuing to Karbala, burial at the Imam Reza shrine in Mashhad due 9 Jul. **New Supreme Leader Mojtaba Khamenei did not attend the Iraq processions** and was reportedly advised against travelling to Mashhad over fears of being targeted — his continued public absence (tied to war-first-day injuries) remains the single biggest unresolved signal of who is actually directing Iran's response through the 8-9 Jul escalation.
- **Positions:** US — Trump's rhetoric hardened further on 9 Jul (a further strike wave, still floating a Kharg Island takeover); pre-midterm calendar less visibly restraining. Iran — foreign ministry/mediators reportedly still want the MOU held; IRGC acting with wide unilateral latitude and has now struck four additional states in one day; Mojtaba Khamenei's continued absence is the key open question. Israel — not bound by the Iran MOU, continuing Lebanon/Gaza operations. Pakistan/Qatar/Egypt/Turkey/Saudi — active crisis mediation continuing through 9 Jul. Kuwait, Qatar, Bahrain, Jordan — newly and directly targeted by Iranian retaliation on 9 Jul, a material widening of the conflict's footprint. China — bilateral Hormuz transit arrangements; the Golestan rail-bridge strike also now directly affects a China/Russia trade link.
- **Open questions:** Does the multi-country retaliation on 9 Jul draw Kuwait, Qatar, Bahrain or Jordan more directly and durably into the conflict? Does the US actually move on Kharg Island? Does Mojtaba Khamenei reappear publicly once the funeral period ends, and does that change Iran's posture? Phase 2 nuclear timeline looks very unlikely to hold within its original 60-day window.
- **10 Jul — pause and diplomatic scramble:** First 24h window without new reported strikes since the 7-8 Jul collapse began. Trump (Truth Social): "The Islamic Republic of Iran has asked us to continue 'talks.' We have agreed to do so, but the United States has stated to them, in no uncertain terms, that the Cease Fire is OVER!" — talks continue even as the truce itself is declared over, no formal termination filed either side. US officials said Washington expects Iran to shortly issue a public statement declaring Hormuz open and guaranteeing no attacks on commercial vessels; Qatari negotiators in Tehran on both the navigation dispute and the wider MOU; Qatar FM reaffirmed mediation commitment; Pakistan also engaged. Despite the diplomatic movement, Hormuz traffic stayed frozen — Lloyd's List: no vessel >10,000 dwt has transited the US-coordinated Southern Highway with AIS on since 7 Jul (LNG tankers still passing). Oil eased on the reopening prospect: Brent -0.5% to $75.92, WTI -0.96% to $71.39 (10 Jul), both still up ~5-6% for the week.
- **New variable — Trump assassination-plot intelligence:** Israel shared intel with Washington that Iranian hardliners, reportedly including new IRGC commander Ahmad Vahidi, want to assassinate Trump. US officials say this reflects hardliner desire rather than an approved operational plan; some suspect Israel is shaping Trump's calculus on further strikes. Unverified but a live wildcard on the fragile pause.
- **Jordan aftermath:** Analysis pieces (10 Jul) frame Iran's strikes on Al Azraq as exposing Jordan's difficult position hosting US/allied forces while trying not to become a combatant.
- **11 Jul — Mojtaba Khamenei breaks his silence with a revenge vow:** His first public statement since the funeral period ended — posted on X, saying Iran has compiled a target list over the 28 Feb strike that killed his father, and "vengeance... must inevitably be carried out." Resolves the "will he reappear" open question, but sharpens the "who is actually directing Iran's response" one: a revenge agenda now sits awkwardly alongside his own foreign ministry's Oman diplomacy.
- **11 Jul — Trump's "decimate" threat and new Treasury sanctions:** After mourners at Khamenei's funeral were seen with "We Will Kill Trump" banners (tied to the Israeli-sourced assassination-plot intelligence flagged 9-10 Jul), Trump threatened on Truth Social to "decimate and destroy" Iran, citing "1000 Missiles... Locked and Loaded." Treasury separately sanctioned Dubai-based Iranian national Ali Ansari (accused of managing a Khamenei/IRGC-linked asset network) and several Iranian exchange houses.
- **11 Jul — Iran privately calls the Hormuz attacks a "mistake":** US officials say Iranian representatives have told Washington the 7 July attacks on commercial shipping were carried out by an internal faction opposed to negotiations, not sanctioned policy, and that Tehran wants talks to continue. Washington's condition remains a public Iranian statement that Hormuz is open to commercial traffic; Iran has not issued one, wanting to retain control of the strait and toll-charging rights. Araghchi met Oman's FM in Muscat on safe-transit mechanisms.
- **Hormuz traffic, 11 Jul:** Still well below the 120-140/day pre-war baseline, moving mainly via naval-escorted convoys; tanker war-risk insurance remains many multiples above pre-crisis levels with several P&I clubs withdrawn. No confirmed precise vessel-count update beyond Lloyd's List's 10 Jul figure (no >10,000 dwt vessel transiting the Southern Highway with AIS on since 7 Jul).
- **12 Jul — GFS Galaxy attack and IRGC's formal Hormuz closure declaration:** IRGC drone-struck the Cyprus-flagged container ship GFS Galaxy transiting the strait on what it called an unauthorised route, disabling it (fire, engine-room damage) and leaving 1 of 11 Indian crew missing (10 rescued). IRGC then declared the strait "closed until further notice, and until the end of American interventions in this region." Iran's Persian Gulf Strait Authority separately said transit "was not possible at present." The US, CENTCOM and Trump directly disputed the closure; Trump told NBC "it's open, we bombed the hell out of them last night." Iran's government said the fresh US strikes had "rendered futile" recent diplomatic efforts — the sharpest rhetorical break from the "talks continue" framing of 10-11 Jul.
- **12 Jul — Third round of CENTCOM strikes this week:** ~140 Iranian military targets hit Sunday (missile/drone sites, naval assets, ammunition storage, coastal surveillance/comms), bringing the week's cumulative total past 300 targets. Iranian state media reported only fragmentary casualties (e.g. a telecoms worker, a navy officer) with no consolidated toll.
- **12 Jul — Iran's barrage widens to five Gulf states:** Qatar (Al-Udeid US airbase — IRGC claimed a destroyed command centre/aircraft-maintenance facility; 3 wounded incl. 1 child by interception shrapnel), Kuwait (Patriot battery, ammo depot, radar site — explosive drones), Bahrain (US comms system, radar site), Oman (drones on several Musandam-exclave sites, on the strait itself), UAE (air defences engaged incoming missiles/drones). Qatar's first hit since April and the UAE's first since early May — both previously spared as mediator-adjacent/less-exposed states — mark the widest expansion of the conflict's footprint since the war began.
- **Oil, 12 Jul:** Brent rose to roughly $78/barrel, WTI to roughly $73/barrel on the closure declaration and ship attack — still below full war-premium levels but reversing the 9-10 Jul pullback.
- **13 Jul — formal War Powers notification, disputed legal footing:** The White House formally notified Congress that US military action against Iran had resumed, starting a fresh 60-day War Powers Act clock. The administration's position: the original ceasefire/MOU terminated hostilities, and Iran's tanker attacks opened a new conflict requiring a new notification and a new 60 days. Democrats dispute this has any legal foundation — Sen. Adam Schiff: "any assertion by the Trump administration that he gets 60 more days to act without Congress has no foundation in law." No resolution; sets up the 14 Jul Senate vote.
- **14 Jul — Senate Democrats block the NDAA over the war:** The Senate voted 50-46, almost entirely along party lines, against opening debate on the $1.15 trillion National Defense Authorization Act. Democrats, led by Schumer, object to advancing the bill while the war drags into a fifth month with no clear endgame, and separately to provisions deepening US-Israel military/intelligence integration (incl. a Pentagon official to coordinate US-Israel defence tech). A rare stall for a normally must-pass bill.
- **14 Jul — Trump drops the Hormuz toll threat, blockade resumes, fourth day of strikes:** Trump withdrew the threatened 20% fee on cargo transiting Hormuz but confirmed the naval blockade of Iranian ports would resume at 4pm ET, CENTCOM again framing itself as "guardian" of the strait. US forces struck Iran for a fourth consecutive day: a power plant on Kish Island was damaged (Kish also hosts naval/radar/aviation infrastructure supporting Iran's missile and drone programs, despite being a tourist resort); sites in eastern Bandar Abbas and between Sirik and Tahrouyi were hit; Hengam Island was struck by US projectiles — all Hormozgan province. Iran's IRGC said it struck two "non-compliant" supertankers transiting the strait, and launched missiles/drones at US infrastructure in Bahrain (5th Fleet host) and US outposts in Jordan.
- **14 Jul — conflict spreads to a Yemen-Saudi front:** Yemen's government (Riyadh-backed) struck Sanaa airport's runway to stop a plane carrying a Houthi delegation arriving from Iran from landing; Iran-aligned Houthis retaliated with missiles/drones on Saudi Arabia's Abha airport, ending a four-year Houthi-Saudi truce and declaring the de-escalation phase over, warning Riyadh "must bear the consequences of its aggression." The war's widest geographic spread yet, drawing in an entirely new bilateral front (Yemen vs Saudi) beyond the existing Iran-vs-Gulf-states pattern.
- **Kharg Island:** still not struck/seized as of 14 Jul — the "does the US actually move on Kharg Island" open question remains unresolved; Kish Island (a distinct, smaller Persian Gulf island) was hit instead.
- **15 Jul — fifth day of strikes, tanker hit near Kharg:** US strikes hit an Iranian oil tanker near the Kharg Island export terminal as it tried to skirt the naval blockade. Oil continued climbing on the news.
- **16 Jul — sixth day of strikes, first hits near Tehran, two bridges struck:** CENTCOM's latest wave (concluded 9pm ET Wed) hit command centres, air-defence sites, missile/drone capabilities and coastal surveillance — including targets close to Tehran itself for the first time this round — plus the Bandar Abbas-Kahurestan-Lar bridge, the Gariveh bridge (Hormozgan) and Iranshahr Airport. Iran's Health Ministry: 35 killed, 300+ injured cumulative since 7 Jul; Hormozgan governor's office: 2 killed, 4 injured from the bridge strikes specifically. Hormuz traffic fell to just 13 merchant ships on Wednesday per Kpler (vs the 120-140/day pre-war baseline). Trump is reportedly again considering seizing Kharg Island outright.
- **16 Jul — Trump's bridges/power-plants ultimatum and Iran's "crush" warning:** Trump told Fox News (Tue) that unless Iran negotiates, "next week comes the power plants... next week comes the bridges." Iran's top military command warned Thursday it would "crush" regional infrastructure if that threat is carried out, reasserting Hormuz as an "unbreakable red line" against US interference — while reportedly weighing asking the Houthis to threaten closing the Red Sea route too if Trump follows through.
- **16 Jul — Vance publicly breaks with elements of Israel's government:** In an interview aired Wednesday, VP JD Vance said "some people" within Israel's government were "manipulating and trying to change American public opinion to keep the war going on indefinitely," citing Time reporting that Trump's former campaign manager Brad Parscale was linked to an Israeli-government-funded campaign paying conservative influencers to denounce the ceasefire; Vance told those responsible to "go to hell." The sharpest public daylight yet between the two administrations over the war's course.
- **15-16 Jul — House GOP's $95bn war-budget resolution and internal split:** House Republicans unveiled a $95bn budget resolution — up to $60bn Armed Services (largely Iran war), $13bn Intelligence, $12bn Agriculture (war-driven farm/fertiliser cost relief), $10bn tied to the SAVE Act — but the party is split over the lack of deficit offsets on a razor-thin majority, a second legislative front (alongside the NDAA blockage) where the war strains the GOP's own agenda.
- **Yemen-Saudi front, 17 Jul:** Having ended their truce with Saudi Arabia 14 Jul, Iran-aligned Houthis threatened fresh strikes on Saudi oil facilities Friday — the front opened 14 Jul continues to escalate rather than settle.
- **17 Jul — seventh night of strikes, bridges and a port tower hit:** CENTCOM struck Iran again, hitting bridges at the southern port city of Bandar Khamir, additional energy sites, and collapsing a tower at a key Iranian port. Iran's Health Ministry: 38 killed (3 women, 1 child), 400+ injured, 47 hospitalised, since the campaign resumed in late June; IRNA separately said 7 died in the Bandar Khamir strikes alone.
- **17 Jul — Iran's retaliation widens to six countries in one night:** IRGC missiles hit Jordan, Kuwait, Bahrain, Qatar, Oman and Syria — the widest single-night spread of the war. Kuwait's a desalination/power plant was hit directly (fire, damage to a large number of generation units; ~90% of Kuwait's drinking water is desalinated) — the first time Gulf-state civilian water infrastructure has been directly damaged. Qatar sounded shelter warnings twice; falling interceptor debris wounded a child.
- **Oil and Hormuz, 17 Jul:** Brent ~$85/barrel (+0.9%), WTI ~$79.80 (+1%), both ~+12% for the week — a one-month high. Hormuz crossings fell to 8 on 16 Jul (Kpler), the lowest in three weeks, with 7 of 8 using the Iranian-controlled route rather than the US-backed corridor as shippers reassess risk.
- **18 Jul — first US combat deaths since the April truce:** Iranian ballistic missiles hit Jordan's Muwaffaq Salti air base (hosts US F-15s/F-22s/F-35s/MQ-9 Reapers), killing 2 US service members and leaving a third missing — the war's first American combat deaths since the truce, taking the cumulative US military death toll to 16. Four more Americans were medevac'd to Jordanian hospitals and later discharged. IRGC claimed it destroyed the base's command-and-control centre and drone hangars; Jordan said it intercepted most of roughly 10 missiles fired. Trump told Fox News strikes would "continue until I say it's enough."
- **18 Jul — CENTCOM's eighth night, first use of fighter jets this round:** In response, CENTCOM struck Iran for an eighth consecutive night, for the first time in this phase using fighter aircraft (not just standoff weapons) to reach targets ~650km inland at Yazd in central Iran — a marked geographic and capability escalation from the coastal/strait-adjacent targeting of nights 1-7.
- **18 Jul — Iran declares the MOU's commitments mutually suspended:** Iran's Deputy FM Kazem Gharib Abadi said the US had "violated and suspended all its commitments" under the Islamabad MOU and that Tehran had "likewise suspended all our own commitments" — the clearest statement yet that the ceasefire framework no longer binds either side. No formal termination notice filed by either side.
- **18 Jul — Rezaei's "complete destruction" ultimatum:** Mohsen Rezaei, adviser to the Supreme Leader and former IRGC commander-in-chief (no longer holds operational command), warned on Iranian state TV that if US strikes continue another "two or three days," Iran will move from retaliatory to "offensive and complete destruction," with "no political border... safe." Broadcast by state media (IRIB) as a signal despite his authority to set such a deadline being unclear absent confirmation from the still publicly-absent Mojtaba Khamenei. Clock (set 17 Jul) runs out roughly 19-20 Jul.
- **18 Jul — Kuwait hit a second consecutive night:** A power/water desalination plant caught fire and lost generation units; an army barracks was struck (several injured); a Kuwait Petroleum Corp oil facility suffered "significant material damage"; Kuwait International Airport briefly suspended flights. GCC Secretary-General Jasem Mohamed Albudaiwi said Iran's strikes on civilian sites in Bahrain, Kuwait and Jordan amount to "war crimes."
- **18 Jul — casualties and oil:** Iran's Health Ministry: 50 killed (5 women, 2 children) and 500+ injured since 6 Jul, incl. 12 killed in the latest 24h alone; 28 surgeries performed, 460 discharged, 37 hospitalised. Brent crude rose above $87/barrel (highest since June), +14%+ for the week.
- **19 Jul — third US death in 48 hours, cumulative toll now 17 confirmed:** A US service member was killed and another injured in northern Iraq during a controlled detonation of unexploded ordnance from a downed Iranian drone (CENTCOM). This is distinct from, and additional to, the two Jordan deaths announced 18 Jul. Separately, the US recovered unidentified human remains at the Muwaffaq Salti site while searching for the soldier still missing from the 18 Jul Jordan strike; identification pending — not yet added to the confirmed toll. CENTCOM completed an eighth consecutive night of strikes on Iran's coastal surveillance, air-defence, missile/drone-storage sites, framed explicitly as punishing the IRGC for the US deaths.
- **19 Jul — Israel warns of direct spillover risk:** Israel's military told residents to expect their first air-raid sirens in weeks after Iranian missiles fired at Jordan's Aqaba risked spilling across the nearby Israeli border — a new, distinct spillover vector from Israel's earlier active role in the war (which has focused on Lebanon/Gaza, not direct Iranian fire at Israeli territory from the Jordan front).
- **19 Jul — Iran's hardliners accuse Ghalibaf/Pezeshkian/Araghchi of a 'coup':** With Mojtaba Khamenei still not publicly reappearing, hardline lawmakers and clerics who attended his father's funeral in large numbers escalated accusations that acting leaders Ghalibaf (chief negotiator), President Pezeshkian and FM Araghchi are executing a political coup — elevating the Supreme Council for National Security over parliament and the Supreme Leader's office, suspending parliamentary sittings, and moving to disperse hardliner street rallies, all while continuing Oman-mediated diplomacy hardliners oppose. Analysts read this as: Mojtaba's continued absence means Ghalibaf and allies are effectively running the country.
- **19-20 Jul — Rezaei ultimatum window closes without confirmed action:** Rezaei's 17 Jul warning (continued strikes for "two or three days" → shift to "offensive and complete destruction") reached the outer edge of its stated window (19-20 Jul) with no confirmed shift in Iranian tactics reported as of this update — watch whether it is acted on in the next 24-48h, quietly dropped, or repudiated.
- **19 Jul — Saudi nuclear-enrichment deal delayed, war-adjacent:** A draft US-Saudi civil-nuclear accord that would let Saudi Arabia enrich uranium without full IAEA Additional Protocol safeguards remains unsigned by Trump; people briefed say the Iran war's optics are among the reasons for the delay, alongside possible bipartisan disapproval-resolution risk in Congress. Tangential to the main MOU arc but part of the same broader Iran-nuclear-file backdrop.
- **Oil, 18-19 Jul:** Brent held near one-month highs, above $87/barrel as of 18 Jul (+14%+ for the week); no confirmed newer figure found in-window (Sunday trading thin).
- **20 Jul — Pezeshkian declares "full-scale war":** President Masoud Pezeshkian said Iran and the US are now fighting a "full-scale war." CENTCOM struck a ninth consecutive night (Tabriz — believed to hold underground missile bases — and Bushehr, Iran's only civilian nuclear plant), with further explosions reported early Monday in Hormozgan (Bandar Abbas, Qeshm, Bemani district). Confirmed US toll held at 17, unchanged from 19 Jul. Rubio said the US remains "open to diplomacy... if that door opens, we'll be happy to see it open," but "their behaviour has to change in order for ours to change"; Tehran's Baghaei said mediators had passed messages between the two sides.
- **20 Jul — Mojtaba Khamenei's first public statement since March, but still no in-person appearance:** A written statement (Saturday 18 Jul) accused the US of "bullying, hegemony and savagery" and vowed "unforgettable lessons," while separately urging "sacred unity" and warning critics not to "cause a fracture in national unity" — an oblique, non-specific response to the hardliner "coup" accusations against Ghalibaf/Pezeshkian/Araghchi rather than a direct rebuttal or endorsement. He has still not been seen or heard from directly since taking the role.
- **20 Jul — Houthis declare a maritime embargo on Saudi Arabia:** Yemen's Iran-aligned Houthis vowed to close the Bab el-Mandeb strait (~12% of world trade) to Saudi-bound shipping, calling it "an eye for an eye" for Saudi Arabia's decade-long "blockade" of Sana'a, following the collapse of their four-year truce (14 Jul) after mutual Sanaa/Abha airport strikes. Saudi-led coalition says it has taken steps to protect Red Sea shipping. Widens the war's economic footprint to a second major chokepoint alongside Hormuz.
- **19-20 Jul — Rezaei ultimatum window closes, no confirmed shift in tactics:** Consistent with the 20 Jul report; no further developments found.
- **21 Jul — first concrete ceasefire proposal since the collapse:** Regional mediators (reportedly Qatar, Egypt, Pakistan) presented Washington and Tehran a 10-day ceasefire proposal to reopen both Hormuz shipping lanes (southern Omani route free of Iranian attacks; northern Iranian route free of the US blockade) while the two sides negotiate a longer-term strait arrangement. Trump administration reportedly exploring it and has urged Israel not to take steps that would close the window, but is said to want a longer ceasefire than 10 days. No agreement confirmed as of 21-22 Jul.
- **21 Jul — CENTCOM's tenth consecutive night:** Struck command centres, maritime-attack capabilities, missile/drone sites and air defences from the Hormuz littoral to Shiraz and Isfahan. A tanker was hit by an unidentified projectile ~8nm off Oman's Kumzar, crew abandoned ship; Dynacom Tankers reported two more of its vessels hit by projectiles off Oman. Kuwait's power/desalination plants were hit again (second time in under a week), fires extinguished. CENTCOM says it has escorted ~900 commercial vessels/450m barrels of crude through Hormuz since early May.
- **21 Jul — Hegseth's Senate testimony:** Told the Senate Appropriations Committee (amid protester interruptions) the war has cost US$37.5bn so far, up from earlier estimates, as the administration seeks funding atop the $95bn GOP war-budget package. Confirmed US military death toll holds at 17; nearly 100 injured this month, 96% returned to duty. Pentagon named the two soldiers killed at Muwaffaq Salti (incl. 1st Lt Tyler James Feehan, 25).
- **21 Jul — Houthi blockade becomes concrete disruption:** Two tankers that had just loaded Saudi crude for China/India at the Red Sea port of Yanbu reversed course toward the Suez Canal rather than risk Bab el-Mandeb, the first tangible shipping impact of the 20 Jul embargo declaration.
- **21 Jul — Israel readies contingency plans but stays on the sidelines:** Netanyahu convened a limited security-cabinet meeting Monday night to review scenarios that could draw Israel in (US request, Iranian trigger, pre-emptive strike); Finance Minister Smotrich said Israel currently has "no interest in joining the campaign."
- **22 Jul — Trump formalises the bridges/power-plants threat into an automatic retaliation rule:** After an 11th consecutive night of CENTCOM strikes, Trump posted on Truth Social that from now on, any Iranian attack on a ship in Hormuz — by missile, rocket, drone or "any other device or weapon" — will be met with the US bombing and destroying "ONE BRIDGE OR POWER PLANT," explicitly including targets "next to, or in," Tehran. This converts the 14 Jul ultimatum (thread 53) into a standing formula rather than a one-off warning. Confirmed US toll holds at 17 killed, now with at least 420 wounded (one still missing since 18 Jul). Iran's FM Araghchi reiterated an "eye for an eye" doctrine, warning any infrastructure strike "will compel a powerful and decisive response"; IRGC Aerospace Force commander Majid Mousavi separately warned that hitting Iran's power stations would black out "the allies and hosts of the child killers" — an explicit threat against US regional hosts.
- **22 Jul — Congress moves on war funding:** House Republicans passed a go-it-alone $95bn war-budget resolution 216-214 (party-line) — $60bn defence, $13bn intelligence, $12bn other — and separately passed their own $1.15 trillion NDAA (renaming the Pentagon the "Department of War," troop pay raises) despite bipartisan objections to Section 219 (deeper US-Israel defence-tech integration); Rules blocked a floor vote on stripping it. Both bills still face a Senate that already blocked its own NDAA over the war on 14 Jul.
- **22 Jul — oil and Hormuz-bypass infrastructure:** Brent jumped 3.4% to $94.10/barrel, the war's highest close yet. Dubai's DP World signed a 50-year concession with Fujairah Ports Authority to build two new deepwater terminals on the UAE's Indian Ocean coast, bypassing Hormuz entirely (up to 30 months to build; lifts UAE container capacity from 19.4M to ~22M TEUs) — the clearest market signal yet that shippers are planning for a multi-year, not short-term, Hormuz disruption.
- **22 Jul — US-Saudi nuclear deal signed, adjacent to but shaped by the war:** US Energy Secretary Chris Wright and Saudi Energy Minister Prince Abdulaziz bin Salman signed a peaceful nuclear cooperation + safeguards agreement giving Saudi Arabia a path to enrich its own uranium (30-year term, tens of billions of dollars; still needs congressional review). Resolves open thread 19 (deal had been delayed partly over Iran-war optics as of 19 Jul). Israeli officials voiced concern the deal risks a wider Mideast arms race, given Iran's own 440.9kg 60%-enriched HEU stockpile.
- **23 Jul — Trump conditions the signed Saudi deal on Abraham Accords/Israel recognition:** A day after signing, Trump said the pact takes effect only if Saudi Arabia joins the Abraham Accords and normalises with Israel — a condition not mentioned at signing. Riyadh has long said it won't normalise without a credible Palestinian-statehood pathway, an unmet precondition given ongoing Gaza operations; analysts call it characteristic Trump dealmaking (sign, then attach fresh leverage).
- **23 Jul — Houthis strike Saudi tankers, oil tops $100, war's economic footprint widens to Bab el-Mandeb:** Houthi rebels struck two Saudi-flagged tankers (Encelia, Layla) in the Red Sea, setting Encelia ablaze (crew safe), enforcing their declared blockade of Saudi ports (thread 1f/45). Brent hit $101.10 intraday (+6.45%), first close above $100 since May, ~+40% for the month; at least 5 Saudi tankers reversed course. Goldman Sachs warns of $120+ Brent by Q4 if disruption continues. CENTCOM completed a 12th consecutive night of strikes on Iran (maritime, missile/drone storage, coastal surveillance, air defence); Iran state media reported 2 killed/11 injured in a Shalamcheh border strike.
- **23 Jul — House votes a second time to end the war, Senate again blocks debate:** House passed a War Powers resolution 214-208 (4 Republicans — Davidson, Fitzpatrick, Barrett, Massie — joined Democrats) directing Trump to end hostilities against Iran; symbolic/non-binding, the House's fifth such vote and second passage this war. A parallel Senate effort failed to advance hours later.
- **23 Jul — Pentagon casualty-transparency dispute:** NYT reporting that the Pentagon's official casualty website understates the true US toll reignited bipartisan senator pressure on Hegseth to fully account for troops killed/wounded since his 21 Jul testimony (confirmed 17 dead, $37.5bn cost); true current toll unconfirmed pending an authoritative update — do not treat aggregator-site tolls (militaryspend.org, iranwarlive.com) as reliable, per 22 Jul precedent.
