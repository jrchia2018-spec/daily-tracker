- **1 Sep — Iran answers Larak with missiles and drones on US bases in Jordan and the UAE; Trump vows to "hit Iran hard" and posts an AI video of Kharg Island burning:** The IRGC mounted a combined missile-and-drone operation it named "Punishment of the Aggressor" against the King Hussein and Al Azraq bases in Jordan, both hosting US forces; Jordan said it intercepted eight missiles shortly after the IRGC's announcement. Iran also claimed to have targeted US military facilities in the UAE — the UAE air force said it responded to a drone detected over its territorial waters approaching from Iran but denied Al Minhad air base itself was struck. No US casualties were reported in either country; Iranian state broadcasters said the 30 Aug Larak strike killed or wounded both IRGC personnel and civilians, with no independently verified figure. This is the first two-way exchange of fire since late July, ending the pause in both directions rather than only the American one (thread 472's open question). Trump told Fox News the US would hit Iran hard in return and posted an AI-generated video to Truth Social appearing to depict the bombing of Kharg Island — the terminal handling the bulk of Iran's crude exports, floated as a target since July and still never struck. US officials indicated the Larak launchers may have been preparing a previously unseen type of Iranian naval mine, a claim that stands directly against Trump's own 28 Aug "MISSION ACCOMPLISHED 2026" demining declaration (thread 156). Brent crude rose 2.93% Monday to US$90.69/barrel, back above US$90 for the first time since the strike pause began, erasing the three-day slide on reopening optimism; 6-8 million barrels/day still move through the strait. On the economic track, the rial hit 2 million to the US dollar on 23 Aug (a ~47-fold devaluation in two years), inflation was 87.9% in data released late July, unemployment reached 9.1% in the spring, GDP is forecast to contract 5.4% in 2026, and central bank governor Abdolnaser Hemmati said in August that Iran is essentially no longer exporting oil — the military and financial tracks now running simultaneously rather than one substituting for the other. Separately, President Pezeshkian attended the 26th SCO summit in Bishkek (31 Aug-1 Sep) with a Putin bilateral scheduled — run as its own global story on the bloc rather than folded into the Iran story, since the summit's substance is China's leverage over Russia and India rather than the MOU; per the consolidation rule, Iran's own sanctions predicament stayed in this arc.
- **2 Sep — the US resumes strikes across southern Iran over an alleged mine-laying attempt and a disputed mined supertanker; Iran says four died at a wedding and fires back:** CENTCOM began striking IRGC targets inside Iran at noon ET Tuesday 1 Sep, the largest American action since the strike pause held through most of August, saying the strikes answered recent IRGC attempts against commercial shipping in Hormuz and against US service members in the region but declining to say what specifically it was trying to destroy. Iranian state-linked outlets (Tasnim, Nour News) reported explosions at Bandar Abbas, Qeshm, Sirik, Konarak and Chabahar. Trump framed the strikes as retaliation both for Iran's attempt to lay fresh sea mines and for the eight ballistic missiles fired at a US base in Jordan on 31 Aug (thread 160), and again extended his threats to Kharg Island, still never struck. Iranian state media said four people including a child were killed and dozens injured when a strike hit a house in southern Iran where a wedding was being held — not independently verified and not addressed by CENTCOM; the IRGC said "several of our fighters and compatriots" were killed and wounded, and Iranian news agencies said retaliatory missile and drone attacks on US targets had begun within hours, with no independent confirmation of damage inside the window. **The trigger is a contested fact, not a contested term:** on Monday 31 Aug Iranian state TV, citing the IRGC, said a supertanker had caught fire and been halted after striking two naval mines in the southern strait, warning other vessels breaking its passage rules would "face the same fate" and naming neither ship nor crew; CENTCOM publicly called the report false and a disinformation campaign meant to intimidate commercial shipping. Both claims sit against Trump's own 28 Aug "MISSION ACCOMPLISHED 2026" demining declaration (thread 156). Brent crude rose 0.87% to about US$91.28/barrel, a second session above US$90. This resolves thread 161's "does Washington follow through on the 'hit Iran hard' vow" question — it did, within 48 hours — and reopens the nightly-strikes-versus-sanctions-only question that the late-July-to-late-August pattern had appeared to settle.
- **3 Sep — the disputed tanker acquires a name and a death toll; Iran calls the wedding strike a war crime and retaliates across four countries; the IAEA says it can no longer locate Iran's HEU:** Saudi Arabia said the vessel struck in Hormuz late on Monday was the *Sidr*, owned by its national shipping company Bahri, and that two Filipino seafarers were killed at 23:40 local time; Riyadh attributed the attack to Iran and condemned the targeting of a commercial ship. The IRGC's account is unchanged and incompatible: it says two tankers struck sea mines while transiting without its authorisation. CENTCOM had already called the Iranian mine claim false and a disinformation campaign; both versions still contradict Trump's 28 Aug demining declaration (thread 156). This is the first named vessel, owner and confirmed death toll in the mining dispute, and it draws in two states previously outside it — Saudi Arabia, whose cargo and ship it was, and the Philippines, whose nationals died. On the Iranian side, officials put the Kuhestak (Hormozgan) wedding-house strike at four to five dead and 68 injured, naming four-year-old Amir-Ali Karimi and sixteen-year-old Mohammad Mollahi among the dead; foreign ministry spokesman Esmaeil Baghaei called it a "war crime" on X and vowed a response "with firmness". The US denied targeting civilians; the account remains independently unverified. Regional outlets put the total Iranian dead from the American barrage at eleven. Iran retaliated overnight despite Trump's warning it would be "hit again at a much harder and higher level": the IRGC fired ballistic missiles at US positions in Jordan including a Marine facility near Aqaba — Jordan's defence ministry counted thirteen missiles entering its airspace aimed at the King Hussein and Al Azraq bases, all landing in remote areas without damage — with missile and drone attacks also reported on US facilities in Bahrain, Kuwait and Iraq. Iran claimed American service members were killed; no independent confirmation in window. Separately the IAEA circulated an eleven-page restricted report in Vienna saying it can no longer verify the location or status of Iran's near-bomb-grade uranium: inspectors have had no access to Fordow, Natanz or Isfahan since June 2025, where 440.9kg of 60% HEU and 8,599.6kg of lower-enriched material were last observed, and Grossi wrote that the lack of information "is a matter of proliferation concern", urging readmission "with the utmost urgency". Brent crude closed at about US$94.86/barrel, +0.23% on the day, a near six-week high and +13.24% over the month (+40.33% year on year). This confirms the answer to 2 Sep's "sustained campaign or single punitive round" question as at least a two-way exchange continuing into a second night.

Saudi Arabia, which withheld its endorsement of the route over Houthi attacks, intercepted further missiles overnight. Brent rose about 3.4% to roughly US$106.60 and the US 10-year yield touched 5.163%, with the 30-year at its highest since June 2004 and the 30-year fixed mortgage rate at 7.26%.

- **26 Sep — the timetable itemised, and the Gulf hedges:** Abbas Araghchi set out the day-by-day sequence behind Tehran's offer: within four to five days Washington lifts the naval blockade, restores the oil-export sanctions waiver, ends the war on all fronts and releases frozen assets; on day six Iran reopens Hormuz on the route agreed with Oman; on day seven nuclear talks begin, pulled forward from the June memorandum's 60 days. The terms are “nothing more than what was already in the Islamabad agreement”, he said — only the speed is new. He added that he doubts Steve Witkoff can deliver, having twice been told talks were productive days before US bombing, and Marco Rubio said Tuesday's meeting was no breakthrough. Masoud Pezeshkian said the US midterms have never figured in Iranian thinking and called supreme leader Mojtaba Khamenei “completely healthy” after seven hours in his presence, with no proof of life issued. Saudi Arabia intercepted six Houthi ballistic missiles aimed at Taif and the Yanbu oil area; Macron is sending soldiers, radars and air defences to protect Yanbu, “not to engage in any conflicts”; and Riyadh, Ankara and Islamabad called an urgent chiefs-of-staff meeting under their joint defence pact. Oil rose about 3% to a one-week high before easing, ending the week more than 2% up. Separately, classified documents given to Congress carry a US intelligence assessment that Saudi Arabia has not ruled out developing nuclear weapons.

- **27 Sep — Trump rejects the seven-day plan outright:** Asked outside the White House what he thought of Tehran's offer, Trump said "I reject their proposal", adding that Iran wants the strait open immediately "because they're losing so badly" and that "that deal would not be acceptable" — no counter-offer was made. Araghchi said on Telegram that Iran has seen the public reaction but will decide only on a formal answer delivered through mediators, and will not retreat from its conditions; a White House official called the mediated talks "positive and constructive" and said that with US control of the strait "we are not in a rush", echoing Rubio's line that any deal means "a lot of hard work over a period of time". The Wall Street Journal reported Trump told staff a renewed bombing campaign is likely after the midterms, with officials saying his view could still shift. Separately, US intelligence assessments described to CNN say Chinese satellite imagery and geospatial data have materially improved Iranian targeting — enabling the July strike on a US base in Jordan that killed three service members, and letting unmanoeuvrable heatseeking drones be timed against ships at a Hormuz "knuckle"; officials cannot say whether Beijing ordered or merely tolerated it. Brent settled about US$105.70 on Friday, -0.88% but some 2% up on the week; the rejection landed after the close.

- **28 Sep — rejection, then an expectation of talks:** A day after saying "I reject their proposal", Trump told Axios he expects negotiations to resume this week — "They want to make a deal, but it is not the deal that I want to make" — adding that Tehran's terms were ones Washington might have accepted about a year ago and that Iran had overplayed its hand; Qatari mediators are shuttling messages, replacing the New York corridor as the venue. Abbas Araghchi told NBC News Iran is "fully prepared for the war to be resumed" and would stand firm "even if it comes to a doomsday war" while keeping indirect talks open ("Diplomacy can never fail"), said the choice is Trump's, said Iran still awaits a definitive answer through mediators, and claimed UN ambassador Mike Waltz had not read the proposal; Waltz said Trump doubted Iran was coming in good faith. Brent traded above US$106 and WTI near US$94 as Monday's Asian session priced the rejection for the first time, Tehran having held to its seven-day terms.

- **29 Sep — the offer's two money terms surface, then are disavowed:** A US official told CNN Trump is willing to grant sanctions relief and release frozen Iranian funds in return for "concrete progress" on the nuclear file, with "positive and constructive discussions through the mediators" but no deal unless the nuclear question is settled first — the sequence reversed from Tehran's seven-day plan, which puts relief before talks. Hours later Trump posted on Truth Social that the reports were "untrue", leaving mediators with two contradictory US positions in one day. Supreme Leader Mojtaba Khamenei, still unseen in public since February, issued a written statement on state television saying enemies who had suffered "painful blows" from "the guardians of the Strait of Hormuz" now "do not venture beyond the Arabian Sea" and that it "will not be long" before that sea too is rid of them. Brent settled at US$105.28 (+96 cents) after an intraday US$108.83; WTI settled at US$92.60. Lebanon accused Israel of setting off large explosions in southern villages, Israel saying it had destroyed more than 600 "terrorist infrastructures" — the Lebanon front Tehran's offer requires be closed. Separately, five British men bailed over a suspected plot at RAF Fairford, the base used by US bombers against Iran, remain under investigation for possible hostile-state proxy involvement; Iran's London embassy denies any link.

- **30 Sep — the chokepoint depreciates:** Saudi Arabia resumed crude loadings at Yanbu and Al Muajjiz after restarting the East–West pipeline, shut since 11 Sep by drone attacks Riyadh blamed on Iraqi militias; loadings run about 2m bpd against 7m of capacity, and satellite imagery showed all seven berths working. Kpler put Hormuz plus bypass routes at nearly 80% of pre-war volumes with Saudi, Iraqi and Emirati output near 13m bpd, the most since February, as tankers run dark and transfer ship-to-ship in the Gulf of Oman; Brent settled US$102.59 (-US$2.69, -2.6%) and WTI US$89.38 (-US$3.22, -3.5%). Araghchi met Qatari mediators and said he hoped for Washington's definitive answer to the seven-day plan; a US official said there is no deal unless the nuclear file is settled first, reversing Tehran's relief-before-talks sequence.
