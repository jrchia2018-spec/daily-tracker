No new casualty, diplomatic or strike-escalation event found beyond these incremental figures; watch for Iran's formal response to the reparations counter-demand (thread 296) or any fresh CENTCOM strike activity.
- **12 Aug — Trump claims "100% control" of Hormuz hours after a third ship is disabled trying to breach the blockade:** Trump told reporters the US Navy has swept Hormuz clear of mines and holds "100%" control — "It's open now," a "steel wall," with Iranian mine-laying merely "on occasion." Hours later a US Navy MH-60 helicopter fired two Hellfire missiles into the Panama-flagged M/V Vela Nova's engine room after it ignored warnings while trying to reach an Iranian port (~71nm off Pakistan); CENTCOM said it is the third vessel disabled since the blockade resumed 14 Jul, alongside two boarded and 55 redirected. - **10 Aug — Trump counter-demands reparations from Iran, Hormuz hopes fade:** After the SNSC's six conditions (thread 287) included "full compensation" for war damage, Trump posted on Truth Social that he is "likewise demanding compensation from Iran" — naming the families of the 17 US sailors killed in the 2000 USS Cole bombing (an al-Qaeda attack, not Iran's), "thousands of others killed in combat," families of "hundreds of thousands" of Iranian protesters killed over 50 years, and "the 52,000 that have been killed in the last five months"; separately said "Iran should be responsible for the damages and death caused to the people of Lebanon, Syria, Yemen, and Gaza!" and has told negotiators to press the claim in all future talks. Iran's Baghaei repeated that Hormuz stays closed until Washington meets Tehran's conditions. Brent rose to ~$87.72/barrel (+5%) as markets read the dueling reparations claims as pushing a signed deal further away, reversing the prior week's cautiously optimistic framing.
- **1 Aug — Kuwait hit again, Iran threatens Gulf energy fields, US embassies warn of "unforeseen escalation":** Kuwait's air defences downed a fresh wave of Iranian drones and a tanker was struck off Hormuz for a second consecutive day. Iran threatened to strike other Gulf states' own oil/gas fields if the US launches a further large-scale attack. US embassies in Bahrain, Egypt, Iraq, Israel, Jordan, Kuwait, Lebanon, Oman, Qatar, Saudi Arabia and the UAE jointly urged citizens to "consider departing, or be prepared to depart should there be escalation" — the broadest coordinated evacuation advisory of the war. Trump, still at Camp David, said "we just want to win" and vowed to keep hitting Iran "very hard... until they can't take it anymore." Brent held near $88/barrel, little moved by the warnings — markets still reading containment rather than an imminent supply shock.
- **1 Aug — Qeshm Island bomb identified as a 2,000lb Mark-84:** New reporting (satellite imagery + munition-fragment analysis) found the crater from the strike that killed a husband, wife and 2-year-old son at a Qeshm Island family home (the "Jafari family" deaths Iran cited as its stated reason for the 31 Jul Kuwait drone claims) consistent with a US Mark-84 2,000lb bomb, one of the largest conventional munitions in the US arsenal. CENTCOM's statement on the "heavy wave" of strikes that included the Qeshm hit did not acknowledge civilian casualties. Sharpens the civilian-casualty question the Pentagon casualty-transparency dispute (thread 101) has already raised about official strike disclosures.
- **1 Aug — Gaza strikes continue despite the Hamas disarmament deal; Lebanon demolitions draw UN alarm:** Israeli strikes on Gaza continued through the weekend despite Thursday's 15-point Hamas disarmament agreement and Trump's public calls for a halt; an Israeli government spokesperson said there is "no ceasefire in place, only a temporary halt in certain bombings" pending genuine Hamas disarmament — underscoring the scepticism flagged in the 31 Jul report. Separately in Lebanon, Israel detonated roughly 700 tonnes of explosives destroying what it called Hezbollah "terror tunnels" near the UNESCO World Heritage-in-Danger-listed Beaufort Castle; the UN said the "scale of detonations and demolitions... is deeply concerning" and called for the "escalating pattern of destruction" to stop.
- **2 Aug — Trump cancels a planned joint US-Israel strike on Iranian energy infrastructure, citing "outlines" of a deal:** After US media reported a joint US-Israel attack on Iranian energy targets was imminent, Trump said on Truth Social he had agreed to Middle Eastern allies' (led by Saudi Arabia, fearing a wider war) request to cancel it, "subject to being able to rapidly make a DEAL" whose outline includes "Immediate, Complete, and Total OPENING OF THE HORMUZ STRAIT, and an end to Iran's nuclear threat," with Israel committed alongside him. The reversal reportedly caught Israel off guard and undercuts Netanyahu's days-old claim of being in lockstep with Trump on Iran — the sharpest US-Israel daylight on Iran policy since the 16 Jul Vance remarks (thread above). No signed agreement exists; this is a mutual pause plus a stated outline. Trump said US-Iran talks resume Monday 3 Aug. Iran's response was guarded: FM Araghchi warned Saudi, Pakistani and Turkish counterparts that any resumed US/Israeli strike would draw a "proportionate response"; President Pezeshkian said Iran "must strive to compel the enemy to honor what it signed" (the original MOU); Araghchi separately said Iran-Oman Hormuz talks are in their "final stage." This is the most consequential de-escalation signal since the war reignited in early July, but remains fragile and unsigned — supersedes thread 202 (Iran's Gulf-energy-field threat/embassy evacuations).
- **2 Aug — Gaza's worst single-day toll in weeks; Israel-Hezbollah ground clash in Lebanon:** Israeli strikes killed at least 18 Palestinians across Gaza City, Deir al-Balah and Khan Younis Sunday — the highest daily toll in weeks — with a Deir al-Balah apartment strike targeting two Hamas "Nukhba" commanders; Israeli Energy Minister Eli Cohen said there is "no deal to halt attacks" and Israel (already controlling 70% of Gaza) should take full control. In Lebanon, IDF troops killed several Hezbollah members overnight in a ground encounter on the Ali al-Taher Ridge (~6 miles north of the border), wounding one IDF officer — a rare direct ground clash rather than a strike, which Netanyahu framed as response to Hezbollah's "blatant violation" of the ceasefire days earlier. Both fronts show the Iran-specific pause has not extended to Gaza or Lebanon.
- **Oil, 2-3 Aug:** Brent fell as much as 7.3% to $81.55/barrel, WTI near $80, on the strike-cancellation news — unwinding much of July's ~25% run-up in a single session, the market's clearest vote yet that the Iran track specifically (not the Gaza/Lebanon violence) drives the war-risk premium.
- **3 Aug — Trump calls Iran "duplicitous" after Tehran denies direct talks; Oman-Hormuz "temporary route" talks reach "final stage":** Trump told reporters renewed talks are Iran's "last chance" to sign a Hormuz-reopening/denuclearisation deal, saying he faces no personal time pressure since he isn't seeking re-election; negotiations would proceed in two phases (strait first, denuclearisation second). Iran's foreign ministry then said Tehran was holding no direct talks with the US at all — only with Oman, on a "temporary" Hormuz shipping route (one path, separate entry/exit lanes) that FM Araghchi called nearly complete. Trump responded on Truth Social branding Iran "extremely duplicitous." No signed US-Iran agreement exists; the 2 Aug strike-cancellation remains a mutual pause on a stated outline, not a ceasefire.
- **3 Aug — oil extends its slide:** Brent fell a further ~4.7% to ~$83.81/barrel Monday (some trackers had it lower, ~$82.92, -5.7%), a second consecutive sharp drop, as markets read the Oman-Hormuz progress as more market-moving than the unresolved US-Iran talks dispute — unwinding most of July's ~25% run-up in two sessions.
- **3 Aug — Gaza: Board of Peace envoy presses Netanyahu, Israel strikes anyway, "serious security concerns" shared with Washington:** Nikolay Mladenov (Board of Peace high representative for Gaza) held "constructive and detailed" talks with Netanyahu and separately pressed him to halt strikes while the 15-point Hamas disarmament deal (thread 190) is implemented. Israel struck Gaza anyway — the first strike in a day, defying the Board of Peace's pressure — killing a man IDF identified as Jalal Sobieh, a Hamas Daraj-Tuffah Battalion commander; IDF said the target was "advancing attacks" on troops/civilians. Netanyahu's spokesman confirmed Israel shared "serious security concerns" about the deal's published terms with the US, saying the public version "does not reflect Israel's positions" — a rift alongside the weekend's cumulative Gaza toll of at least 26 dead (Fri-Sun).

Iran's FM spokesman Baghaei repeated that Tehran holds no direct US talks, only Oman-mediated shipping-route talks — consistent with 2-3 Aug. Brent crude swung sharply on the comments: fell $3.95 (4.72%) to $79.82/barrel by mid-afternoon GMT, from an earlier $86.33, briefly touching $79.73 (~5% down), but held above $80 by the close, a sign traders aren't yet pricing out the risk premium entirely.
- **4 Aug — US missile-interceptor stockpiles reported "dangerously low":** CNN reported the US has burned through ~80% of its pre-war THAAD interceptor inventory and roughly half its Patriot interceptors since the war resumed — fewer than 827 Patriot and 278 THAAD interceptors left — with commanders warning of readiness risk extending beyond this war (China/Russia/North Korea contingencies); replenishment could take years given production lead times.
- **4 Aug — continued Red Sea/Gulf shipping risk despite Hormuz-talks optimism:** an India-flagged cargo vessel, MSV Faize Noore Oliya, capsized and sank after being hit by a projectile in Yemeni waters; all 14 crew (13 Indian) rescued by the Yemeni coast guard, taken to Port of Mokha; India's MEA condemned the strike. Shows the broader regional maritime-security crisis persisting alongside the Hormuz-specific diplomatic track.
- **5 Aug — Iran and Oman finalise Hormuz shipping-route coordinates, reopening still contingent on the US blockade:** After roughly two months of technical/legal/security/environmental talks, Iran's foreign ministry said Iran and Oman reached agreement on the geographic coordinates of a proposed Hormuz shipping route, with a joint statement in final drafting; spokesman Esmail Baghaei called the talks "professional" and progressing "provided that certain third parties do not obstruct the process," but stressed the reopening remains contingent on the US removing its naval blockade and halting attacks on Iranian infrastructure — a bilateral Iran-Oman deal alone cannot guarantee safe navigation while the blockade stands. Trump said a deal could come as early as Wednesday; a Gulf official told CNN the odds were roughly "50-50" of a signed agreement by Friday. Brent held essentially flat, -0.3% to $79.15/barrel, a muted follow-through on 4 Aug's sharper 5.3% drop — markets discounting rhetoric until a signed deal (and blockade withdrawal) actually materialises.
- **5 Aug — Israel resumes Lebanon strikes mid-Rome-talks, first evacuation warning in weeks:** Israel struck southern Lebanon Wednesday, accusing Hezbollah of an unspecified ceasefire violation without providing evidence, and issued its first evacuation warning in over a month (Mansouri, ordering residents ≥1,000m north); Lebanon reported at least one dead. The strikes landed mid-way through the seventh round of US-brokered Israel-Lebanon Rome talks on a phased Israeli withdrawal; Israel's delegation (led by US ambassador Yechiel Leiter) cut the session three hours short, blaming Lebanese "misleading leaks," with both sides still divided over a second buffer zone, compliance verification and private-property inspections. Brent held near $83.64/barrel.
