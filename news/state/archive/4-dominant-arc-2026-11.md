- **1 Nov — the Iran front is quiet in-window for the third time in five days, and the pricing of it moves instead:** No verified new strike, Hormuz incident or diplomatic movement between 31 Oct and 1 Nov 0700 SGT. The 30-31 Oct limpet attack and the IRGC's disputed mine claims (reported variously as three and four tankers; CENTCOM calls such claims false) stand as last covered. Transits have fallen from a pre-crisis baseline of about 85 vessels a day to a handful on some days, with Gulf crude exports still averaging 15.7m bbl/day in the week to 26 Oct. Seven OPEC+ producers meet Sunday 1 November, outside the window; previews expected a hold. Any US strike is still briefed as post-3 November.
- **1 Nov — the Binance theft becomes a state act and is substantially made good:** The FBI attributed the US$6.3bn to TraderTraitor, the same North Korean unit it named for Bybit's US$1.5bn in February 2025, putting government attribution behind TRM Labs, Chainalysis and Elliptic. Binance says it has replaced ~90 per cent by buying new tokens with company funds, set aside ~US$6.34bn internally, and was on track to reopen withdrawals on Sunday. Freeze figures conflict: Binance ~US$3bn (over US$2.3bn by Tether and Circle), TRM ~US$2.7bn with ~US$2.4bn still held by the attacker.
- **1 Nov — a US court suspends the federal pressure campaign on election administration three days before the vote:** Judge Trina Thompson (N.D. Cal.) granted a TRO on Friday evening barring DOJ and DHS from demanding states segregate ballots, purge rolls or halt processing, and from threatening or coercing election officials; lawful administration, she held, is not a federal crime even where the government alleges a voter ineligible. The order runs to 16 November, compels disclosure of warrants and subpoenas tied to the DHS data-matching lists, and was appealed by DOJ on Saturday. Brought by unions, the NAACP and LULAC under the Voting Rights Act. Over 50m early votes are already cast.
- **1 Nov — the European cost-of-living revolt becomes transnational:** 320,000 marched across France (70,000 in Paris) in a third act after 17 and 24 October, with clashes in Paris, Marseille, Lyon and Toulouse; Nuñez threatened fines for masked faces and Macron denied weighing platform suspensions. A new German youth movement, "Gen Z regelt", drew tens of thousands in Berlin, Frankfurt, Cologne and Munich against living costs, school and university funding, environmental policy and a possible return of conscription, with water cannon in Cologne and Hamburg and Molotov cocktails at emergency workers in Frankfurt. Anti-government protests also swept Italy.
- **1 Nov — two deferred settlements, one environmental and one historical:** COP17 closed in Yerevan with the "Yerevan Pathway" — national plans to be revised "subject to available funding", acceleration towards the existing US$500bn-a-year harmful-subsidy cut by 2030, criteria but no structure for a post-2030 finance mechanism, voluntary donor-base broadening, GEF and GBFF reviews, and an invitation to China and other wealthy emerging economies to become donors; US$1.8bn announced in total and US$60.13m pledged to the GBFF. Separately UK PM Andy Burnham opened the door to a formal state apology for the Atlantic slave trade while ruling out reparations, before the Antigua CHOGM; Browne and Mottley welcomed talks without moving their positions and CARICOM called it disappointing.
- **1 Nov — carried, not run:** Poland's tribunal crisis entering a second day (riot police deployed, Tusk defending the overhaul in a prime-time address, Nawrocki squaring off) — incremental on 31 Oct. Judge Stearns blocking the CMS "medically frail" definition under the Medicaid work requirement for 25 states and DC (ruled 29 Oct, reported 30 Oct 1412 EDT = 31 Oct 0212 SGT, just outside the window). Ethiopia's 12 Russian Su-35s and four S-400 batteries (announced 28 Oct at the Russia-Africa summit; Rosoboronexport says only two jets delivered) against renewed Tigray fighting. - **2 Nov — the Iran arc moves to a 48-hour bargain in Doha, and the price is American:** A third straight day of indirect US-Iran talks ran in Doha on 1 Nov, Qatari mediators having met Iranian officials in Tehran on 30 Oct before the venue moved. Araqchi called the talks "ongoing and progressing", said the "two-day proposal" had reached no conclusion and that no meeting with US representatives is planned: day one Washington lifts the oil-shipping blockade, stops blocking the asset release and ends oil sanctions; day two Hormuz reopens and traffic returns to its former state. Qatar has compressed Iran's original seven-day timetable to five and is pressing for two, and is brokering release of more than US$6bn of the ~US$12bn of Iranian money held in Doha; Gulf officials are "braced for the worst" awaiting Washington's answer, possibly before the 3 November midterms, with Trump holding to no strikes before the vote and Rubio saying talks "have actually never stopped". Fourth quiet day in six — no verified strike or Hormuz incident in window.
- **2 Nov — the European revolt turns violent and transnational:** Saturday's Gen Z regelt marches in Germany broke into rioting overnight. Hesse's Roman Poseck reported 17 shops damaged and five looted in Frankfurt, 1,300 officers deployed, more than 100 injured and 54 arrests of people aged 13-29, alleging ~100 identified left-wing extremists had infiltrated; water cannon were used in Berlin, Cologne and Hamburg, where NDR counted 120 arrests. In France, after 320,000+ marched on a third national date, prosecutors opened an attempted-murder investigation into petrol bombs thrown at riot police; demonstrations also ran in Switzerland, Portugal and Albania. Conscription sits in the German demand list beside living costs, school and university funding and spending cuts.
- **2 Nov — Ukraine ends both pauses at once:** The Kherson humanitarian ceasefire, in effect since 27 Oct and the longest local pause of the war, expired at 22:59 Ukrainian time on 1 Nov; Kyiv evacuated 167 people that day (22 children, 21 with limited mobility) and the ombudsman declared the Oleshky mission over, with continuation under discussion. Kyiv also ended the ~two-week refinery pause Washington sought: Novak confirmed three refineries and a gas-chemical complex hit, including Rosneft's Novokuybyshevsk and Kstovo, with Fire Point acknowledging both; Moscow opened a terrorism case and the Kremlin said Kyiv was "simply ignoring" US wishes. Russian strikes on energy infrastructure in four regions had left hundreds of thousands of homes without power.
- **2 Nov — the pre-emptive certification challenge fails a second time:** Judge Sharon Gleason (D. Alaska) dismissed on 1 Nov the DOJ suit seeking to bar certification of Alaska's Senate result if incumbent Dan Sullivan loses, a case built on a second ballot-listed Daniel J. Sullivan Jr. and voter confusion; she held too many ballots were already cast to establish intent. Trump said he ordered the suit personally, DOJ will appeal, Sullivan said he will accept the result, and DOJ is separately investigating the alleged plot to add the second name. It is the second such defeat in three days after Judge Thompson's TRO, and an appellate panel now holds both.
- **2 Nov — India's electoral roll becomes an opposition mass issue:** Tens of thousands filled Ramlila Maidan in Delhi on 1 Nov under "Vote Bachao, Desh Bachao", with leaders of nearly two dozen INDIA-bloc parties including Rahul Gandhi, Mamata Banerjee and Akhilesh Yadav demanding CEC Gyanesh Kumar resign over the Special Intensive Revision and alleging "vote chori". Gandhi put deletions since the general election at ~13.5 crore (135m), unverified by any independent audit; the BJP called the rally an attack on a constitutional institution, police reported no serious incident, and a mooted impeachment motion against Kumar has not been filed.

- **3 Nov — the channel becomes American and the war premium is repriced:** Axios reported Vice-President JD Vance had written to Iranian national security chief Mohsen Rezaei about new talks, the highest-level US move in months, while Araqchi said mediated consultations were “very intensive” and that “everything depends on the political rationality of Washington”; reports also said Supreme Leader Mojtaba Khamenei had granted his government “powers” to negotiate. Terms are unchanged — the 48-hour swap, with Qatar brokering more than US$6bn of the ~US$12bn held in Doha. Brent fell more than 6.5% to US$105.55 (lowest since 16 Oct) and US crude more than 7%; no strike or Hormuz incident was verified in window, a fifth quiet day in seven, and Trump told an Iowa rally Iran would get “the hammer” without progress.
- **3 Nov — the Kherson pause ends in a bombed aid queue:** At least nine FAB-3000 bombs hit a school in Komyshuvakha, Zaporizhzhia region, used every Monday for aid distribution, the second wave striking the shelter; SES put the toll at 24 (two rescuers, four police, five injured), Reuters at 26, with 27 reported missing. The 27 Oct Kherson ceasefire expired at 22:59 on 1 Nov with no violation and fighting resumed within hours; Kyiv says Moscow refused an extension and fortified positions, which Peskov called “a completely normal practice” while praising the Oleshky mission.
- **3 Nov — Europe's food chain is attacked a second time in a month:** Refrigeration in supermarkets across at least seven European countries (Belgium, Luxembourg, the Netherlands and Sweden named) was driven to 50C overnight; Ahold Delhaize reported dozens of stores, Carrefour Belgium ~15 of 700, Intermarché 15, Colruyt 5, with ICA and Ikea also hit and evaporators switched to defrost mode. EU commissioner Christophe Hansen called it “a deliberate attempt to disrupt our food supply chain”; Sweden's cyber chief cited exposed operational-technology devices and the US Army is investigating similar disruptions. It follows October's attack on as many as 700 French supermarkets, with fires and losses in the tens of millions of euros.
- **3 Nov — the pre-election legal campaign loses at the top:** The Supreme Court refused, in a one-sentence order with no reasoning and no noted dissents, the Solicitor-General's emergency bid to set aside ballots from 20,000+ Californian registrants, hours after it was filed and three days after the Ninth Circuit's 2-1 refusal. The same day Magistrate Judge Zia Faruqui dismissed for want of probable cause the false-statements case against former FBI officials Nikki Floris and Bradley Benavides, calling it “weaponisation” and “marketing material for people not to join the FBI”, with testimony that Kash Patel ordered the investigation the day after a Trump primetime address. Third government defeat in five days.
- **3 Nov — Britain reprices permanent residence by salary:** Mahmood told the Commons settlement “must be earned”: under £50,000 earners wait 10 years not five, above £50,271 and doctors/nurses/teachers five, above £125,000 three; refugees 10 years, +10 for illegal entry, up to 30 if not working, none eligible if they have drawn public funds. A care-worker carve-out (over £25,000, arrived before May 2025) lets ~280,000 people and dependants apply after five years. Applies retrospectively to more than half a million people, needs no vote; the Law Society cited loss of legal certainty and Abbott warned of a second Windrush.
- **3 Nov — carried, not run:** Trump's removal of South Africa and Rwanda from AGOA effective 1 Jan 2027 after the annual review (South Africa exported ~US$1.7bn under the programme in 2025; one estimate adds 3.4% to existing 12.5% tariffs) — the strongest held item and the first to run on any further development. The first US avian-influenza death of 2026 (Yakima County, Washington; third since Feb 2024; 33 infected dairy herds since 8 Oct; 70+ human cases since 2024, no human-to-human transmission). Fake nuclear-attack warnings pushed through hacked Russian and Ukrainian emergency apps. Binance withdrawals resuming with US$3.6bn taken out, and Pyongyang calling the FBI attribution “absurd rhetoric”. Netanyahu and Eisenkot both bidding for the first coalition mandate before the 4 November certification. Uganda beginning its withdrawal from Somalia after 20 years. William Nordhaus's death. CISA's retreat from election-security work.
