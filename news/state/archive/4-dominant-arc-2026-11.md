- **1 Nov — the Iran front is quiet in-window for the third time in five days, and the pricing of it moves instead:** No verified new strike, Hormuz incident or diplomatic movement between 31 Oct and 1 Nov 0700 SGT. The 30-31 Oct limpet attack and the IRGC's disputed mine claims (reported variously as three and four tankers; CENTCOM calls such claims false) stand as last covered. Transits have fallen from a pre-crisis baseline of about 85 vessels a day to a handful on some days, with Gulf crude exports still averaging 15.7m bbl/day in the week to 26 Oct. Seven OPEC+ producers meet Sunday 1 November, outside the window; previews expected a hold. Any US strike is still briefed as post-3 November.
- **1 Nov — the Binance theft becomes a state act and is substantially made good:** The FBI attributed the US$6.3bn to TraderTraitor, the same North Korean unit it named for Bybit's US$1.5bn in February 2025, putting government attribution behind TRM Labs, Chainalysis and Elliptic. Binance says it has replaced ~90 per cent by buying new tokens with company funds, set aside ~US$6.34bn internally, and was on track to reopen withdrawals on Sunday. Freeze figures conflict: Binance ~US$3bn (over US$2.3bn by Tether and Circle), TRM ~US$2.7bn with ~US$2.4bn still held by the attacker.
- **1 Nov — a US court suspends the federal pressure campaign on election administration three days before the vote:** Judge Trina Thompson (N.D. Cal.) granted a TRO on Friday evening barring DOJ and DHS from demanding states segregate ballots, purge rolls or halt processing, and from threatening or coercing election officials; lawful administration, she held, is not a federal crime even where the government alleges a voter ineligible. The order runs to 16 November, compels disclosure of warrants and subpoenas tied to the DHS data-matching lists, and was appealed by DOJ on Saturday. Brought by unions, the NAACP and LULAC under the Voting Rights Act. Over 50m early votes are already cast.
- **1 Nov — the European cost-of-living revolt becomes transnational:** 320,000 marched across France (70,000 in Paris) in a third act after 17 and 24 October, with clashes in Paris, Marseille, Lyon and Toulouse; Nuñez threatened fines for masked faces and Macron denied weighing platform suspensions. A new German youth movement, "Gen Z regelt", drew tens of thousands in Berlin, Frankfurt, Cologne and Munich against living costs, school and university funding, environmental policy and a possible return of conscription, with water cannon in Cologne and Hamburg and Molotov cocktails at emergency workers in Frankfurt. Anti-government protests also swept Italy.
- **1 Nov — two deferred settlements, one environmental and one historical:** COP17 closed in Yerevan with the "Yerevan Pathway" — national plans to be revised "subject to available funding", acceleration towards the existing US$500bn-a-year harmful-subsidy cut by 2030, criteria but no structure for a post-2030 finance mechanism, voluntary donor-base broadening, GEF and GBFF reviews, and an invitation to China and other wealthy emerging economies to become donors; US$1.8bn announced in total and US$60.13m pledged to the GBFF. Separately UK PM Andy Burnham opened the door to a formal state apology for the Atlantic slave trade while ruling out reparations, before the Antigua CHOGM; Browne and Mottley welcomed talks without moving their positions and CARICOM called it disappointing.

Judge Stearns blocking the CMS "medically frail" definition under the Medicaid work requirement for 25 states and DC (ruled 29 Oct, reported 30 Oct 1412 EDT = 31 Oct 0212 SGT, just outside the window).
