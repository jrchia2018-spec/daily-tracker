## Threads opened 1 Nov 2026
- **A state act, mostly paid for.** The FBI named TraderTraitor for Binance's US$6.3bn; Binance replaced ~90 per cent from company funds and set aside ~US$6.34bn internally. Watch whether withdrawals reopen as promised, whether any indictment or sanctions action follows the attribution, and whether exchange custody is moved into regulated territory.
- **An order with an expiry date.** Judge Thompson's TRO bars DOJ and DHS from coercing election officials until 16 November; DOJ appealed within a day. Watch whether the Ninth Circuit leaves it in force through 3 November, whether the DHS data-matching lists survive the other cases examining them, and whether any official is charged after it lapses.
- **A revolt with a shared calendar.** 320,000 marched in France on a third date, a new "Gen Z regelt" movement filled four German cities, and Italy joined. Watch whether a fourth coordinated date is called, whether conscription becomes the German movement's central demand, and whether any government concedes on cost-of-living measures rather than policing.
- **Targets without a treasury.** COP17's Yerevan Pathway makes revised national biodiversity plans conditional on available funding and defers the post-2030 finance mechanism to criteria only. Watch whether China or another wealthy emerging economy accepts donor status, what the GEF and GBFF reviews conclude, and whether the US$500bn subsidy-cut target gets a single national schedule.
- **An apology offered, reparations refused.** Burnham will hear calls for a formal state apology for the slave trade but rules out payments, before the Antigua CHOGM. Watch whether the summit communiqué records an apology, what "practical help" is quantified as, and whether CARICOM's 15 states accept the split.
- **Compliance by persuasion.** Five haze-noticed companies agreed to engage assessors, Kencana Agri is willing and First Resources has said so publicly; Bumitama Agri, TSH Global Plantation and TSH Oversea have not. Watch whether the last three agree or force NEA to use the Act's powers, and whether self-appointed assessors produce findings anyone treats as conclusive.
- **Half the island covered.** Project Wolbachia reached 840,000 households (53 per cent) in its tenth year, with Tampines down from ~1,200 dengue cases in 2020 to 77 in 2025. Watch whether DENV-3's rise in Q3 2026 breaks the suppression record, and whether NEA commits to coverage beyond 53 per cent.
- **Nine years of reasoning, released at once.** The Employment Claims Tribunal published 17 judgments in 2026, its first since 2017, including a maximum S$30,000 award for a dismissal at the end of probation. Watch whether claim volumes rise, whether the S$30,000 cap is revisited, and whether employers change probation documentation.
- **The oldest town densified.** Toa Payoh West is to take more than 6,000 homes, the town's first mall, a 1.2ha wellness park and possibly a school, starting with the 1,600-unit Toa Payoh Hillside BTO in November. Watch the November launch's application rates, whether the school is confirmed, and what the mall does to Toa Payoh Central.
- **Care moved closer, weekly.** The three clusters upgraded 35 community health posts in 2026, NHG's 27 going monthly to weekly, with 12 more by March 2027. Watch whether utilisation justifies the frequency, whether frailty screening at AACs feeds referrals, and whether the model is costed against avoided admissions.
- **Carried, not run (1 Nov):** Poland's tribunal crisis in its second day — riot police deployed, Tusk defending the overhaul in a prime-time address, Nawrocki squaring off (incremental on 31 Oct; the one to run if charges are filed or the Court of Justice is asked to rule). Judge Stearns blocking the CMS "medically frail" definition under the Medicaid work requirement for 25 states and DC, decided 29 Oct and reported just outside the window. Ethiopia's 12 Russian Su-35s and four S-400 batteries, announced 28 Oct, against renewed Tigray fighting. DR Congo's Ebola outbreak past 5,000 deaths and 10,000 cases. Also held: Syria weighing accession to the Saudi-Turkey-Pakistan mutual defence pact; the completed Oleshky evacuation and reunions; Erin Patterson resentenced to life; Spain's traffic-control attribution and judge-led inquiry for the Urda collision; the Nasdaq's worst month in over a year; Edwin Tong's ICJ remarks (overlapping 31 Oct's Oceans Four story, with the 3 November ICJ vote on Rena Lee still pending); the record S$1.818m Queenstown five-room resale; HDB's review of the jumbo-flat conversion scheme; and rising bone-marrow donor registrations against only ~20 per cent of 500 annual searches ending in a transplant.

- **No-repeat log (covered 1 Nov):** The FBI's TraderTraitor attribution and Binance replacing ~90 per cent of the US$6.3bn; Judge Thompson's TRO against DOJ and DHS pressure on election officials and the Saturday appeal; the 320,000-strong third French mobilisation with "Gen Z regelt" in Germany and protests in Italy; COP17's Yerevan Pathway and the deferred post-2030 finance mechanism; Burnham's openness to a slavery apology with reparations ruled out before CHOGM; five haze-noticed companies agreeing to engage assessors with Kencana and First Resources signalling; Project Wolbachia passing 53 per cent of households in its tenth year; the Employment Claims Tribunal's first 17 published judgments and the S$30,000 award; the Toa Payoh West masterplan and the 1,600-unit Toa Payoh Hillside BTO; and 35 community health posts upgraded in Singapore's first super-aged year.

## Threads opened 2 Nov 2026
- **A bargain with a stopwatch.** Qatar has compressed Iran's Hormuz plan to 48 hours — US measures on day one, reopening on day two — and is brokering more than US$6bn of the ~US$12bn held in Doha. Watch whether Washington answers before 3 November, whether the money actually moves, and whether any reopening is independently verified.
- **A revolt the state can file as extremism.** Hesse blames ~100 identified left-wing extremists for Frankfurt's looting; France has opened an attempted-murder case over petrol bombs at riot police. Watch whether the infiltration account becomes official, whether a fourth coordinated date is called across the five countries, and whether conscription stays in the German demand list.
- **Both pauses ended on the same day.** The Kherson truce expired at 22:59 on 1 Nov and Kyiv resumed refinery strikes against an explicit US request, drawing a Russian terrorism case. Watch whether humanitarian measures are extended, whether Trump imposes any cost on Kyiv, and whether Russian refining throughput falls measurably.
- **Pre-emptive certification, nought for two.** Judge Gleason dismissed the DOJ's bid to block Alaska's Senate result, three days after Judge Thompson's TRO; both are on appeal. Watch what the Ninth Circuit does before Tuesday, whether the Alaska ballot-plot inquiry charges anyone, and whether the instrument is tried in a third state.
- **A roll nobody has audited.** The INDIA bloc filled Ramlila Maidan demanding CEC Gyanesh Kumar resign over the Special Intensive Revision, with Rahul Gandhi putting deletions at ~13.5 crore. Watch whether an independent audit of the deletions is ever produced, whether the impeachment motion is filed, and whether the Commission publishes a reconciliation.
- **A spill with no owner.** MPA confirmed oil patches off Pulau Sebarok, Pulau Semakau and Selat Pauh on 1 Nov and activated its contractors with the source unidentified. Watch whether a vessel or terminal is named, whether shorelines are affected, and who ends up paying for the cleanup.
- **One number, one point of failure.** From 2 Nov every police call comes from 6000-0999 with a gov.sg verification SMS, after government-impersonation losses hit S$142.5m in nine months against S$61.4m a year earlier. Watch whether case volumes fall, whether other agencies follow, and whether scammers learn to spoof the new line.
- **Top-up cover being shed at scale.** 181,800 Integrated Shield riders were dropped or downgraded in 2025, against 103,400 in 2024, with MOH saying it does not track reasons. Watch whether 2026 figures accelerate, whether MOH starts collecting reasons, and whether out-of-pocket claims rise at the restructured hospitals.
- **The Gulf as a precedent for Malacca.** Zaqy Mohamad told the 22nd Manama Dialogue that restricting transit through Hormuz or Bab el-Mandeb could erode navigational rights in the Straits of Malacca and Singapore, citing the IFC's 58 partners and 163 linkages. Watch whether any Hormuz settlement records a transit-rights principle, and whether the IFC's partner count is converted into escort capability.
- **Procedure over the reparations question.** DPM Gan Kim Yong leads Singapore's delegation to CHOGM in Antigua, 2-5 November, with London having agreed structured dialogue on reparatory justice while keeping it off the formal agenda. Watch what the communiqué records, and whether the summit produces any trade or standards outcome for small states.
- **Carried, not run (2 Nov):** The OpenAI/Altman suit over Bowen Wan's death after a GPT-4o lamotrigine taper (filed ~28-29 Oct, outside the window). The G20 Atlanta family photo scrapped over Lavrov (30 Oct). Netanyahu's coalition arithmetic and the Arab-led parties' 19 seats before 4 November certification. Syria weighing accession to the Saudi-Turkey-Pakistan pact. Poland's tribunal crisis with Tusk's prime-time defence. The UK's 10-year settlement rules (announcement due 2 Nov). London's structured CHOGM dialogue on reparatory justice, folded into the Gan story. The WSJ/Epstein dismissal. Senator Chris Murphy's stroke. Nine dead in a Palm Beach County Learjet crash. Kimi Antonelli, F1 champion at 20. Also held: the 1,310-flat Sembawang November BTO launch (near-repeat of 1 Nov's Toa Payoh Hillside item); the government's build-to-rent study (announced 3 Aug, today's coverage was explanatory); at least 20 schools hiring external manpower for student discipline ahead of the 2027 standardised measures; the ST decoy-research figures, which could not be grounded; and the Istana Open House's first inclusive-employment showcase.

- **No-repeat log (covered 2 Nov):** The two-day Hormuz proposal in Doha with Qatar brokering more than US$6bn of Iranian assets; the German cost-of-living riots with 17 Frankfurt shops damaged and the French attempted-murder case; the Kherson truce expiring and Ukraine resuming refinery strikes with Moscow's terrorism case; Judge Gleason dismissing the DOJ's pre-emptive Alaska certification suit; the Ramlila Maidan rally and the demand that CEC Gyanesh Kumar resign over the roll revision; the oil patches off Pulau Sebarok, Pulau Semakau and Selat Pauh; the police switch to the single 6000-0999 caller line; 181,800 Integrated Shield riders dropped or downgraded in 2025; Zaqy Mohamad's Manama Dialogue warning on Hormuz as a precedent for Malacca; and DPM Gan leading Singapore's CHOGM delegation to Antigua.

## Threads opened 3 Nov 2026
- **A channel Washington can own, or drop.** Vance's letter to Rezaei is the first named US approach since 9 July, with Araqchi saying everything turns on Washington's “rationality” and Mojtaba Khamenei reportedly granting negotiating “powers”. Watch whether a direct meeting happens, whether the answer comes after Tuesday, and whether Brent holds below US$110 if it does not.
- **The truce instrument discredited.** Hours after the Kherson pause expired without violation, nine FAB-3000 bombs hit a Monday aid distribution at a Komyshuvakha school, killing 24-26. Watch whether any local truce is attempted again, whether the toll settles, and whether aid distribution schedules are changed or hidden.
- **Refrigeration as critical infrastructure.** A second wave in a month pushed shop freezers in at least seven countries to 50C, with the EU commissioner calling it deliberate and the US Army investigating its own disruptions. Watch whether anyone is attributed, whether the EU brings retail operational technology into its critical-infrastructure regime, and what the two waves cost in total.
- **A one-sentence end to the ballot theory.** The Supreme Court refused the California segregation bid without reasoning or noted dissents, hours after filing; Judge Faruqui separately called the FBI prosecution weaponisation. Watch whether the administration accepts the refusal after the vote, whether Floris and Benavides face any further charge, and whether CISA's withdrawal from election-security work is reversed.
- **Settlement by salary band, applied backwards.** Britain doubled the settlement wait for sub-£50,000 earners to 10 years, set up to 30 for non-working refugees, and exempted ~280,000 care workers and dependants. Watch whether the retrospectivity survives judicial review, whether the carve-out holds, and whether other G7 systems copy the salary bands.
- **The pass and the deployment.** MOM is investigating ~45 Changi firms and ~270 workers over work-pass offences in SATS' contractor chain, including EP holders doing baggage handling on allegedly false declarations. Watch whether charges follow, whether SATS loses or restructures contracts, and whether the sweep extends beyond the airport.
- **Shared plumbing, single point of failure.** A global Advance Passenger Processing outage stopped Changi self-check-in at all four terminals for about two and a half hours on 2 Nov. Watch whether the operator identifies a cause, whether other airports publish their impact, and whether CAG adds a manual fallback standard.
- **The SEZ gets an institution.** The first Johor-Singapore Cooperation Ministerial Committee recorded RM88.83bn (S$28.1bn) approved, 45 per cent implemented and 12,796 of 28,755 projected jobs created. Watch whether the implementation share rises by the Q1 2027 investment forum, and whether the committee keeps publishing figures.
- **The middle path, codified.** MDDI's engagement of 2,000-plus found ~80 per cent of parents and ~70 per cent of youths prefer tiered access to an outright ban, with age-assurance legislation due in 2027. Watch what age bands the bill sets, how age is to be verified and what data is retained, and whether Australia's ban survives its High Court challenge.
- **Seizure is not recovery.** Only five of 47 auctioned properties from the S$3bn laundering case have sold, the latest 13 per cent under guide, with the 60 per cent foreign ABSD and pre-seizure valuations blamed. Watch whether guide prices are cut, whether any ABSD remission is granted for liquidation sales, and what total is eventually recovered against the S$3bn seized.
- **Carried, not run (3 Nov):** South Africa and Rwanda removed from AGOA effective 1 Jan 2027 (~US$1.7bn of South African exports in 2025) — the strongest held item, to run on any further development. The first US bird-flu death of 2026 in Yakima County, Washington, with 33 infected dairy herds since 8 Oct. Fake nuclear-attack alerts pushed through hacked Russian and Ukrainian emergency apps. Binance withdrawals reopening with US$3.6bn taken out and Pyongyang dismissing the FBI attribution. Netanyahu and Eisenkot competing for the first coalition mandate before 4 November certification. Uganda's withdrawal from Somalia after 20 years. William Nordhaus's death. CISA's retreat from election security. Also held: the 73-year-old killed by an SMRT bus in Chinatown; Tanjong Pagar Distripark halting outdoor music over noise complaints; the STI's 1.4 per cent fall before bank earnings; and Parliament's order paper for haze, COE and healthcare-disruption business, which falls outside the window.

- **No-repeat log (covered 3 Nov):** The Vance letter to Rezaei with Araqchi's “very intensive” consultations and Brent's 6.5 per cent fall to US$105.55; the Komyshuvakha aid-point bombing and the Kherson truce's expiry; the second European freezer cyber-attack wave across at least seven countries; the Supreme Court's one-sentence refusal on Californian ballots with Judge Faruqui's dismissal of the FBI prosecution; Britain's earned-settlement rules and the care-worker carve-out; MOM's investigation of 45 Changi firms and 270 workers; the global Advance Passenger Processing outage at Changi; the first Johor-Singapore Cooperation Ministerial Committee and RM88.83bn approved; MDDI's finding that parents prefer tiered social-media access to a ban; and the five-of-47 clearance rate on properties seized in the S$3bn laundering case.


## Threads opened 4 Nov 2026
- **A strike inside the channel, and a price list.** Qeshm was hit while Vance called diplomacy promising, and Rezaei answered with seven conditions and the judgement that the US reply "was not convincing". Watch whether Washington answers the conditions explicitly, whether the two-day window produces direct contact, and whether Brent holds near US$103.60.
- **The memorandum, not the money.** Trump said US$4bn a year to Israel ends on 31 December, two years before the 2018-2028 memorandum expires, claiming an agreement Netanyahu's office did not confirm. Watch whether it lapses on the date, whether Congress intervenes, and whether it survives a renewed Iran campaign.
- **Intelligence on chemical weapons, and who believes it.** Israel passed Washington material claiming Iran is weighing chemical-weapons use; US officials split, some invoking the 2002 Iraq assessments, and Gulf officials were sceptical. Watch whether it is corroborated, withdrawn, or used to justify a strike.
- **Publicity no longer protects aid.** Komyshuvakha reached 33 dead and 50 missing, with the UN mission saying the advertised distribution was either known to Russian forces or not checked for. Watch whether local truces and corridors continue to be attempted, whether the toll settles, and whether distributions stop being announced.
- **Prosecutions that run through polling day.** Target letters went to more than a dozen people mostly aligned with the Democratic party, including Elias Law Group leadership, over the Alaska spoiler-candidate recruitment. Watch whether charges follow, whether the administration accepts the certified count, and whether the Alaska race is contested after the result.
- **A fortnight of unattributed food-chain attacks.** The freezer campaign reached ten countries and British meat processors, with the NCSC warning of a real prospect of an unprecedented rise in incidents. Watch whether anyone is attributed, whether retail refrigeration and airline dispatch enter the EU critical-infrastructure regime, and what the total loss is.
- **Singapore takes a seat on the world court.** Rena Lee was elected first round, 151 of 191 in the General Assembly and 13 of 15 in the Security Council, for 2027-2036. Watch which cases she sits on, and whether Singapore campaigns for a second term in other UN bodies on the back of it.
- **The haze bill, allocated.** Emergency attendances rose a fifth and polyclinics a quarter, 113,000-plus used the S$10 subsidy, employers must absorb short-term suspensions, and platform workers got advisories rather than income protection. Watch whether a platform-worker income measure appears, and whether MOM enforces the suspension rule in inspections.
- **The haze centre's six signatures.** Singapore will ratify the 2002 transboundary haze agreement within days and use its 2027 ASEAN chairmanship to establish the coordinating centre, to sit in Indonesia; Vietnam approved mid-2026. Watch how many of the six ratifications are in place by the chairmanship, and whether Indonesia ratifies the establishment agreement.
- **A quota that stops growing, and bands inside it.** Most consultation respondents backed merging COE Categories A and B with five bands of S$7,500 steps over three of S$15,000, and Siow said the car population cannot grow much further. Watch whether five bands are adopted in the H1 2027 review, and whether the adjustments are fixed sums or a percentage of the premium.
- **The car as a sensor.** From January 2027 every Singapore-registered vehicle must carry an ERP 2 on-board unit, police may use its data in investigations, retention is bound by investigative need, and motorists cannot read it. Watch whether any warrant requirement is added, whether speeding enforcement follows, and whether the retention policy is published.
- **Carried, not run (4 Nov):** AGOA removal of South Africa and Rwanda, held a second day because the in-window report added no action beyond the notification already logged — still the first item to run on any further development. The SIA/Amadeus breach: 46,000 SIA booking records, 620 with contact and travel-document details, no financial data, alongside Malaysia Airlines, Cathay Pacific, JAL, Korean Air and Asiana, covering data processed Aug 2023-Sep 2026; dropped on length and the first SG item to run if it widens. Also held: Spain's transport minister resigning over the 69-death rail crash; Australia's High Court hearing on the under-16 social-media ban; Google pulling Search from schools that reject its AI; a New York child's measles death; US help with a Venezuelan drone strike and the push for a troop agreement; King Charles on Britain's slave-trade role at CHOGM; Poland's border hardening; Singapore's Asia Pacific One Health Initiative; the Courts' S$178,000 PDPC fine and the MoneySmart breach; MOH's apology for the 9.5-hour healthcare IT outage; the Padang UNESCO decision; and Scoot's Middle East cancellations to February 2027.
